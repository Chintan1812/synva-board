STRATEGY BRIEF

SURFACE PAIN: "My hearing aid keeps whistling. Is it faulty? Did I buy the wrong one?"

REAL PAIN (1SD): **Public embarrassment, and the fear that the money is already gone.**
The whistle is not a private malfunction. Other people hear it. It happens in the
moment of affection, hugging a grandchild, leaning towards a spouse, and it announces
the device to a room that had not noticed it. Underneath: *"I paid ₹50,000 to hear
better and now I am the person whose ear is beeping. Maybe I got cheated."*

TARGET VIEWER MOMENT:
Three weeks to three months after purchase. They have stopped wearing it for full days.
It whistles when they put it on, when they chew, when someone hugs them. They have
already tried the two things anyone tries, pushing it in harder and turning the volume
down, and turning the volume down has made the device pointless. They are now quietly
deciding whether this was a waste of money, and they have not called the clinic because
they half-suspect they are doing something wrong and are embarrassed to ask.

FUNNEL STAGE: **Post-purchase / retention** (not Awareness, not Decision). This is the
channel's proven troubleshooting cluster: Bluetooth pairing 13.2 v/day, connecting
without Bluetooth 13.9, "speech still not clear" 11.4. They already own a device.
The consultation pivot is therefore **"bring it in and let us measure it"**, not "buy".

CORE TAKEAWAY:
**It is almost never the machine, and almost never the dome. It is how it is going in.**
Whistling is feedback: sound leaks out of the ear and re-enters the microphone. About
**nine times out of ten the bud is simply sitting loose or was not pushed in properly**
(Chintan, 2026-08-13). Your audiologist already chose the right dome for your hearing
loss, so changing it yourself is not the fix and can make your hearing worse. If a
correct re-insertion does not stop it, the answer is a **10 second measurement** at the
clinic, not a new device.

NOT FOR:
- Anyone whose device whistles **continuously, sitting alone on a table, not in the
  ear.** That is a hardware fault, go to the clinic, this video will not help.
- Anyone with **active discharge, pain or a recent ear surgery.** Fit changes are not
  the answer, an ENT is.
- **CROS/BiCROS users** — different acoustic problem, out of scope.
- People whose whistle appeared **suddenly after years of trouble-free wear.** That is
  usually wax or a receiver failure, and it is a clinic visit, not a dome change.

RECOMMENDED HOOK TEMPLATE: **A — Hidden Variable**
WHY: The viewer believes the variable is the *device* (brand, price, tier). The hidden
variable is the **seal**, a ₹40 rubber dome they have never once thought about. That
gap between "I bought a ₹1 lakh machine" and "a ₹40 part is the problem" is the whole
video, and it is demonstrable on camera in three seconds. It also sets up the honest
reversal: the ₹40 part is exactly the thing you must NOT change by yourself.

### THE CLINICAL FACT BASE (Chintan, 2026-08-13) — this is what the video is built on

**Definition, established up front:** whistling **is** feedback. Establish the word in
the opening definition, then use the patient's word for the rest of the video.

**Cause, in order of how often it actually happens:**

| Rank | Cause | How common |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The bud is loose, or not fitted properly into the ear by the user** | **~90%, say "nine times out of ten"** |
| 2 | Genuinely the wrong size | The rest, and rare |
| 3 | Gain set above what that ear can hold | Needs measurement, see below |

> **Chintan's explicit framing instruction:** do **not** state a hard percentage that
> implies Synva mis-fits devices. Say *"nine times out of ten this happens because of
> this"*, then *"the rest is a wrong size, and that is rare."*

**Dome change is almost never the solution.** Domes are normally installed correctly by
the audiologist according to the hearing loss. If the whistle persists after a correct
re-insertion, the next step is a **Critical Gain Measurement**, not a different dome.

**Where it happens, corrected:**

| Device | Does it whistle? |
|---|---|
| **BTE** | **Most common**, and **most of all in basic hearing aids below ₹30,000** |
| **RIC** | Yes, and this is where **measurement matters most**, see below |
| **Custom in-the-ear (ITE/ITC/CIC)** | Generally **should not happen**, the shell is moulded to that ear |
| **Instant-fit** | **Will not happen** if the device is actually seated in the ear |
| **Any basic device pulled out of the ear** | **Will whistle. That is normal, not a fault.** |
| **Devices with no feedback cancellation feature** | More often |
| **Higher-end devices** | **Very rare**, unless power is very high, i.e. profound losses |

**Solutions, by device:**
- **BTE** → the **correct size dome**; if the loss is **profound**, a **custom mould**.
- **RIC** → many dome options, shown **on screen / as a board**, not demonstrated on a
  person. **Do not tell viewers to change them.**
- **RIC + sloping loss** → **vented domes are deliberate**. They cannot be fully sealed,
  because the vent is what preserves natural sound. This is exactly why **critical gain
  or feedback measurement matters most on RIC.**

**The measurement:**
- Phonak: **"Feedback & real ear test"** (Target). Signia: **"Critical Gain Measurement"**
  (Connexx 9). Same job, different names.
- **It takes literally 10 seconds.** The only real cost is travelling to your audiologist.

**The occlusion trap:** seal a vented dome to kill a whistle and you buy occlusion, your
own voice booming in your head. This is why dome type is a prescription, not a preference.

WEB FACTS (gap-fill, fetched 2026-08-13, 3 queries)

**Phonak's measurement is called "Feedback & real ear test"** (in Target). It produces a
**feedback threshold** that caps the gain available before feedback occurs, drawn in
Target as a **solid purple line when measured, dotted when merely estimated**. The
estimated line does not account for the individual's ear acoustics. The threshold can be
**overtuned by up to 12 dB**. No extra test equipment needed.
Source: https://audiologyblog.phonakpro.com/take-control-in-managing-phonaks-feedback-system/
and https://www.phonak.com/content/dam/celum/phonak/master-assets/en/documents/resources/phonak-target/PH_UserGuide_Target8.1_210x297_EN_058-0125-081.pdf

**Signia's is called "Critical Gain Measurement"** (in Connexx 9), run inside first fit
or via **Fitting → Critical Gain Measurement**.
Source: https://www.signia-pro.com/en-us/sales-tools/connexx/ and
https://forum.hearingtracker.com/t/critical-gain-feedback-management/24579

> **Chintan's recollection was right and it is now verifiable: the two brands name the
> same test differently.** Phonak "Feedback & real ear test", Signia "Critical Gain
> Measurement". Saying both names on camera is a credibility beat no competitor video
> in the set does, and the measured-vs-estimated distinction (solid vs dotted line) is a
> genuinely strong visual for a board.

**Dome types, vendor-sourced:**
- **Phonak: Open · Vented · Closed · Power.** Open is highly ventilated with multiple
  vent openings to reduce the plugged/occlusion effect. Vented is the middle ground,
  tiny openings letting air circulate and unwanted sound escape, minimising the boomy
  voice. Closed has only small vents and offers more feedback control. **Power has a
  complete seal and amplifies even low-frequency sounds.**
- **Signia: Click Sleeves**, closed and vented, in multiple sizes. The **closed** sleeve
  has a 0.8 mm × 4 mm vent, giving an **equivalent vent of 1.6 mm diameter × 16 mm**.
  The **vented** type has two openings giving an equivalent vent of **>4 mm × 16 mm**.
Sources: https://hearwellservices.com/hearing-aid-accessories/domes/phonak/open-closed-power/
· https://japebo.com/signia-click-sleeve-closed/ · https://shop.hearingtracker.com/collections/hearing-aid-domes

> **A correction to fold into the shoot.** Chintan's dictated plan runs dome **size**
> (small/medium/large) and dome **type** (vented/closed/power) together. They are two
> different controls doing two different jobs and a viewer will conflate them:
> **SIZE** decides whether the dome touches the canal wall at all, so a too-small dome
> leaks and whistles. **TYPE** decides how much venting is deliberately engineered in,
> and that is an acoustic prescription, not a comfort preference. Separate them on the
> board: *size = fit*, *type = prescription*. The Signia numbers above make the second
> point concrete, closed 1.6 mm equivalent against vented >4 mm.
